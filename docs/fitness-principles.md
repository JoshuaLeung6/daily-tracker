# fitness-principles.md

Expert-brain reference for the app's design/analysis sessions. One recreational PPL lifter (heavy "weight days" + lighter "volume days"). All numbers verified; contested points flagged in their own section.

## Energy balance & rates

- **Cutting rate:** 0.5–1.0% BW/week, slower half as leanness increases. **Compute the flag from %BW, not fixed pounds** — at 150–200 lb, 0.5–1.0%/wk is ~0.75–2.0 lb/wk. Sustained loss >1% BW/wk is the muscle-loss warning unless significantly overweight (Garthe 2011: 0.7%/wk preserved/gained lean mass; 1.4%/wk didn't).
- **Deficit size:** 300–500 kcal/day default; cap ~500 unless high body fat. >500 kcal/day increasingly costs lean mass in trained people; <500 with lifting + high protein often recomps. 500 kcal/day ≈ 1 lb/week.
- **Muscle retention on a cut is expected, not hoped for**, given: loads kept heavy (volume may drop), protein 1.6–2.4 g/kg/day (upper end dieting), loss ≤ ~0.7% BW/wk. Without training + protein, 20–40% of lost weight can be lean mass.
- **Bulking:** +10–20% over maintenance (~200–500 kcal/day; 200–300 conservative default for a non-beginner). Gain target 0.25–0.5% BW/wk for beginners; intermediates do better at ~0.1–0.25%/wk, advanced 0.05–0.15%/wk — faster gain skews increasingly to fat (~0.38%/wk put only ~60–65% into fat-free mass vs ~85% at ~0.16%/wk). Surpluses >500 kcal/day mostly add fat.
- **Realistic muscle gain:** ~1–2 lb/month year one; ~0.5–1 lb/month intermediate; far less advanced. Scale gain beyond this is water/glycogen/gut/fat — e1RM trend is the better bulk-quality signal.
- **Recomp** is real: best <~2–3 yrs training or >~20% BF (men), or returning from layoff; occurs in trained lifters with 2.0+ g/kg protein at maintenance-to-small-deficit; slows sharply with leanness/training age.
- **Adaptive TDEE:** TDEE ≈ mean intake + (weekly trend-weight change in lb × 3,500)/7 over a ~28-day window; needs 3–4+ weeks of consistent logs; formulas are ±10% starting guesses only. Expect maintenance to drop ~100–200 kcal per 10–15 lb lost — re-estimate continuously.
- **Weigh-ins:** daily, morning, post-void, pre-food; act only on 7-day rolling average/EWMA. Daily noise ±1–2% BW (~2–4 lb; glycogen binds ~3 g water/g). Adjust intake 100–200 kcal only after 2+ weeks off-target.
- **Diet breaks:** 1 week at maintenance every 4–8 weeks of deficit improves hunger/adherence; expect a sustainability benefit, **not** faster loss or better lean retention (ICECAP null in trained lifters). 1–2 day refeeds: weaker evidence, psychologically fine.
- **Cut warning flags:** trend loss >1% BW/wk; protein <1.6 g/kg — both evidence-based lean-mass-risk flags. An e1RM drop >5–10% from pre-diet baseline is a warning **worth investigating** (glycogen/fatigue often explain it), not a validated predictor of muscle loss — strength and lean mass dissociate in a deficit.

*Sources: Garthe 2011; Helms 2014; ISSN 2017; Murphy & Koehler 2022; Iraki 2019; Barakat 2020; Hall/NIH modeling; MATADOR 2018; ICECAP 2021.*

## Nutrition targets

- **Protein:** 1.6 g/kg/day is a reasonable floor capturing most benefit; 2.2 g/kg is a defensible target (not a wasteful ceiling) and trained lifters may do better in the upper half. Cutting: 2.0–2.4 g/kg. Daily total dominates everything else.
- **Distribution (second-order):** 3–5 feedings of ~0.4 g/kg (~25–40 g) every 3–5 h; optimize only after the daily total.
- **Quality:** animal sources reach ~2.5–3 g leucine at ~25–30 g vs ~35–40 g for a single plant source. No strength difference plant vs animal; a real (not trivial) muscle-mass advantage for animal protein exists in younger adults. The leucine "threshold" is a planning heuristic, not a physiological switch.
- **Anabolic window:** wide — protein within ~2 h either side of training suffices (effective window 4–6 h). Only fasted training/two-a-days make immediate post-workout protein urgent.
- **Carbs:** 4–7 g/kg/day is a reasonable planning range, but the acute performance benefit for typical lifting is weaker than folklore (most controlled studies show no acute benefit for sessions on normal daily carbs). No special pre-workout fueling for <60–75 min sessions; 1–2 g/kg 1–3 h prior for >90 min or fasted work. Low carb availability trims volume/rep tolerance before top-set strength.
- **Fat:** floor, not target — never below ~20% of calories or 0.5 g/kg; prefer 0.8–1.0 g/kg. Rationale: satiety, adherence, fat-soluble vitamins, hormone-precursor margin (the testosterone-drop evidence is weak — don't lean on it).
- **Fiber:** 25–38 g/day (14 g/1000 kcal). Benefits are satiety/cardiometabolic/gut — materially helps cut adherence; low intake is a food-choice problem, not caused by high protein.
- **Hydration:** baseline 2.5–3.5 L/day; 5–7 mL/kg 2–4 h pre-training; replace 1.25–1.5 L/kg lost. >2% BW fluid loss clearly impairs endurance and raises RPE; for lifting the hit is modest (~2–3% strength decrement, mainly repeated/later sets). Gym sweat is modest; squash/running are not.
- **Alcohol:** a heavy dose (~1.5 g/kg ≈ 10–14 drinks) cut myofibrillar MPS 24% with protein, 37% with carbs (single acute study). Practical rule is dose + proximity: keep to ~1–2 drinks and avoid training nights.
- **Logging reality:** self-report undercounts 11–41% (assume logged totals 10–25% low; worse in dieters). A logged deficit with no weight change is a logging problem, not metabolism. Consistency beats accuracy — same method daily; adjust ~10% (200–300 kcal) only after 2–3 weeks of trend disagreement. **Log protein carefully (±10 g), calories approximately (±15%)** — protein is chronically under-consumed by default and has no self-correcting signal, whereas calorie error reveals itself in the weight trend.

*Sources: ISSN 2017; Morton 2018; Helms 2014; Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018; Kerksick 2017; Henselmans 2022; ACSM/AND/DC 2016; IOM DRI; Parr 2014; Burrows 2019; Nutrition Reviews 2025.*

## Training & progression

- **Double progression:** fixed load, add reps within a range (e.g. 6–10); when all sets hit the top, add 2–10% load (~+2.5 kg upper, +5 kg lower) and reset. App flags "ready to load."
- **2-for-2 rule:** +2 reps over target on the final set for 2 consecutive sessions → increase load.
- **Hypertrophy volume:** 10–20 hard sets/muscle/week (sweet spot ~12–16); minimum effective ~4. Count only sets at 0–4 RIR.
- **Strength minimum dose:** 1–3 sets at 70–85% 1RM, 2–3×/week preserves/builds 1RM; returns diminish sharply past ~10 sets/week. Busy week → don't skip; 2–3 heavy sets holds progress.
- **Frequency:** 2×/muscle/week preferred as a volume-distribution tool (5–10 sets/muscle/session; cap ~10–12); volume-equated, frequency itself is small-to-negligible.
- **PPL norms:** 6-day = 2× frequency (preferred if recoverable); 3-day = 1× (fine if weekly sets still land 10+); rolling P-P-L-rest ≈ 1.5–2×. Weekly hard sets per muscle is the variable that matters.
- **Heavy/volume days = DUP**, well supported: weight day 3–6 reps @ 80–90% 1RM; volume day 8–15 reps @ 60–75% on the same patterns. DUP matches or slightly beats linear for 1RM; hypertrophy equal.
- **Rep ranges:** hypertrophy is load-independent ~5–30 reps near failure; 1RM strength is load-specific (>80% 1RM). Chase e1RM PRs on heavy days, rep PRs on volume days — never judge a volume day by e1RM.
- **Proximity to failure:** hypertrophy 0–3 RIR (true failure adds little, costs much); heavy compounds 2–3 RIR, never grind maximal squat/deadlift to failure; 5+ RIR doesn't count as a hard set.
- **Epley e1RM** (w × (1 + reps/30)): trust only from ≤10-rep sets (best 3–8, ~±5%; ~2–10% error at 2–10 reps); ignore/de-weight >10-rep sets; compare within similar rep counts.
- **Order:** main compound and any PR-priority lift first (strength ES ~0.3 for first-performed; hypertrophy ~0). An e1RM dip on a lift moved later in the session is an ordering artifact.
- **Stall response:** no rep/e1RM PR in 3–4 weeks despite consistency → add a set, change rep range, or deload. Standard deload: ~1 week at 40–60% volume every 4–8 weeks or on stall.

*Sources: ACSM 2009; NSCA; Schoenfeld 2016/2017/2019/2021; Pelland 2024/25; Androulakis-Korakakis 2020; Robinson 2024; Grgic 2017/2022; Rhea 2004; Nunes 2021; LeSuer 1997.*

## Recovery, plateaus & cardio

- **Plateau detection:** ~3 consecutive stalled sessions (~2–3 wks on PPL, trained consistently) is a sensible **product heuristic, not an evidence-defined threshold** — e1RM carries 5–10% noise, so volume-load at matched load is the more reliable stall signal. Flag earlier (2 sessions) only if performance is declining.
- **Diagnose before reprogramming**, in order: (1) energy availability/weight trending down, (2) protein, (3) sleep, (4) missed sessions, (5) accumulated fatigue. A stall in a deficit is expected — expect maintenance, not PRs, when losing >0.5% BW/wk.
- **Progress expectations by training age:** novices PR near-every session; intermediates should be judged on 4–8 week e1RM trends (monthly gains are variable and often <1% on main lifts — no validated rate exists). Plateau logic must be trend-over-weeks, not per-session.
- **Deload as the lowest-cost first intervention** on a confirmed stall (before changing exercises). Schedule every 4–6 weeks (surveyed athletes: 5.6 ± 2.3 wks) or reactively after 2 consecutive declining sessions. Duration: 5–7 days (surveyed: 6.4 ± 1.7). Prescription: same frequency, ~50% of sets, 60–80% of load, 4+ RIR. Cost: essentially zero for hypertrophy; one trial suggests a small possible cost to lower-body maximal-strength gain — still worth it when genuinely fatigued. Detraining proper takes ~2–3+ weeks off.
- **Overreaching:** no single marker works — act when 2–3 co-occur for ~a week: e1RM down on 2+ lifts across 2+ weeks, rising RPE at constant load, sleep below baseline, mood/motivation drop, unusual joint soreness/illness. Subjective wellness measures are the most sensitive; RHR is a weak supporting signal (see Contested). Functional overreaching resolves in days–2 weeks of reduced load; non-functional takes weeks–months; true OTS (months–years) essentially never happens to a 3-day PPL lifter — treat with a deload, and investigate sleep/calories/illness if fatigue persists past 3–4 weeks.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h general adult guidance; athlete-facing guidance is 8+ h. Flag a 7-day rolling average <7 h. In a deficit, 5.5 vs 8.5 h at identical calories cut fat loss 55% and increased lean loss 60% — sleep is a body-composition lever. Acute sleep loss impairs PM sessions and multi-joint lifts most (magnitude highly variable — don't quote a fixed %); a bad-sleep day means cap the top set, not skip.
- **Cardio interference:** negligible for strength (SMD −0.06) and hypertrophy (SMD −0.01) at recreational volumes; only explosive/power work is measurably affected (SMD −0.28, concentrated in same-session training with <3 h between modalities) — largely irrelevant to this lifter. Conservative guardrails (not established limits): keep hard running modest near leg day; separate hard cardio and lifting by ≥6 h or use non-lifting days; lift first if same session; avoid hard running within 24 h before leg day. Walking/cycling/squash sit comfortably alongside lifting.
- **Steps:** mortality benefit plateaus ~7,000–9,000/day; 10,000 is marketing; target 8,000–10,000 under 60; <4,000 is the real warning zone. **NEAT falls silently in a deficit** — a step decline alongside a stalled weight trend is a diagnosable, fixable pattern (hold steps near baseline while dieting; specific cutoffs like ±1,000 or −20% are tuning parameters, not validated).
- **Baseline activity:** 150–300 min/wk moderate or 75–150 vigorous + 2 lifting days (PPL already covers the latter). Same-muscle spacing of ~48–72 h (lower body longer) is a rule of thumb — recovery is driven more by per-session volume and failure proximity than fixed windows; standard PPL layouts satisfy it. Easy cardio on rest days is fine; complete inactivity isn't required.

*Sources: Bell 2023/2024/2025; Coleman 2024; Meeusen (ECSS/ACSM) 2013; Hirshkowitz 2015; Nedeltcheva 2010; Craven 2022; Schumann 2022; Paluch 2022; WHO 2020; Levine 2015; Sousa/Helms 2024.*

## Contested areas (experts disagree)

- **Protein plateau location:** Morton's 1.6 g/kg breakpoint has a 1.03–2.20 CI; newer trained-lifter data suggest benefit past 1.6. 1.6 defensible, 2.2 defensible, >2.4 unsupported outside hard cuts. Related: total-BW vs fat-free-mass targets; whether ISSN's 2.3–3.1 g/kg-FFM cutting figures generalize beyond contest prep.
- **3,500 kcal/lb:** practitioner convention; true value varies ~2,500–4,000+ with tissue composition. Fine for 28-day regressions, unreliable for multi-month projections.
- **Diet breaks & metabolic adaptation:** MATADOR (obese) positive, ICECAP (lean trained) null — unclear if any benefit beyond adherence generalizes to lean lifters.
- **Surplus size in trained lifters:** "gaintaining" near maintenance vs 10–20% surplus; direct comparative evidence is thin. Similarly: how lean/advanced a lifter can be and still meaningfully recomp; whether >500 kcal/day deficits are acceptable early for high body fat (athlete vs clinical guidance split).
- **Per-meal distribution & the leucine threshold:** long-term distribution effects are weak-to-null; leucine "trigger" may be a modeling convenience. Meal frequency (3 vs 6) shows no metabolic advantage at matched macros.
- **Carbs for hypertrophy:** 3–5 vs 4–7 g/kg both published; whether the volume-tolerance difference matters over months is unresolved.
- **Alcohol at social doses:** MPS mechanism proven only at ~1.5 g/kg; no trial shows 1–2 drinks measurably reduces gains.
- **Failure vs 1–3 RIR:** growth rises toward 0 RIR but several trials find no meaningful difference once fatigue cost is counted.
- **Frequency's causal role:** volume-equated analyses find it negligible — 2×/week may be pure volume-distribution convenience; for a stalled muscle, adding frequency vs adding sets are roughly the same intervention.
- **Upper volume limit:** meta-regressions show growth past 20+ sets/week; practicality for natural recreational lifters disputed. Set-counting conventions for indirect work shift all landmarks.
- **Periodization per se:** DUP-vs-linear advantage is small, lift-dependent, possibly a variation artifact.
- **Deloads:** proactive-scheduled vs reactive-only (RCTs support reactive for non-elite lifters); cut load vs cut sets is untested head-to-head; the 5–10-day duration bounds are heuristics.
- **Plateau definition:** no research-defined stall threshold exists — 2–4 sessions all defended; depends on e1RM noise, training age, deficit status.
- **RHR/HRV for lifter overreaching:** validated in endurance athletes; resistance fatigue often shows no RHR change or even the opposite direction. Soft signal only.
- **Running vs cycling interference:** Wilson 2012 modality effect weakened by Schumann 2022's null; practitioners still hedge toward cycling near leg day.
- **Step targets:** health-minimum (~7–9k plateau) vs weight-maintenance-optimum (maintainers average more) are different numbers, often conflated. Also: whether sleep *extension* beyond 8 h helps strength, and how much fluid people actually need (thirst + urine color vs prescribed liters).

## How these map to the app's data

- **Weight regression:** 7-day EWMA/rolling average is the only weight the app should reason from. 28-day intake-vs-trend regression → adaptive TDEE (needs 3–4 wks of logs; re-run continuously). Flags computed from %BW: loss >1%/wk (cut), gain >0.25–0.5%/wk by training age (bulk = fat-forward). Suggest ±100–300 kcal adjustments only after 2+ weeks of trend-vs-target disagreement. A "deficit with no loss" → prompt logging consistency, then check step count (NEAT drop), before any metabolism narrative.
- **e1RM trends:** compute Epley only from ≤10-rep sets (weight heavier confidence at 3–8 reps); compare within rep bands; exclude lifts moved later in session order from trend judgments; suppress "strength down" alarms on volume days. In a deficit, treat e1RM as a *lagging* indicator — lead with loss-rate and protein flags; an e1RM drop >5–10% from pre-diet baseline triggers an "investigate / consider diet break" prompt, not a muscle-loss verdict. Stall flag: ~3 stalled sessions (label as heuristic), corroborated by volume-load at matched load; "ready to load" when all sets hit the top of range, or via 2-for-2.
- **Weekly volume:** count hard sets (0–4 RIR) per muscle per week against the 10–20 band (~12–16 target, ≥4 minimum, ~10–12 cap/session); track per-muscle frequency; on a confirmed stall with clean recovery markers, offer add-a-set / redistribute-frequency / deload options and schedule deload prompts every 4–6 weeks or on 2 declining sessions.
- **Adherence:** protein tracked to ±10 g of target daily (flag <1.6 g/kg, cutting target 2.0–2.4); calories treated as ±15% and assumed 10–25% under-logged; fiber vs 25–38 g; sleep 7-day average vs 7 h (deficit weeks weight this heavily — it changes what the loss is made of); steps vs personal baseline; overreaching prompt on 2–3 co-occurring soft markers for ~a week; missed-session detection gates all plateau logic.
